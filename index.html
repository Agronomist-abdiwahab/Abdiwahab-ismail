"My work is grounded in one conviction: Somaliland's prosperity grows from healthy soil, resilient farmers, and sustainable land use."

Abdiwahab Ismail Abdi is an agricultural professional working in Somaliland's public sector with experience spanning field extension, crop development, nursery systems, landscape development, and programme coordination.

His portfolio combines practical field delivery with strategic coordination: farmer training, irrigation development, policy support, land restoration, urban greening, and stakeholder engagement with public institutions and development partners.

This website presents his career background, technical areas of focus, selected projects, publications, and contact information for collaboration, consultancy, and professional enquiries.

Thought Leadership

Insights & Articles

Below are three ready-to-publish draft article ideas written for Abdiwahab's profile and grounded in Somaliland's agricultural realities.

Draft Article 01
Building Climate-Resilient Farming Systems in Somaliland
Somaliland's farmers are facing harder seasons, shorter rainfall windows, and rising pressure on soil and water resources. This draft article explains how practical climate resilience can be built through drought-tolerant crop selection, soil moisture conservation, water harvesting, and stronger farmer extension systems. It positions Somaliland not only as a climate-vulnerable region, but also as a place where local knowledge and smart agronomy can work together to produce durable results.
Draft Article 02
Why Nursery Development Matters for Somaliland's Food Security and Greening Agenda
This draft focuses on the strategic value of nursery systems in Somaliland. It covers how quality seedlings support reforestation, urban beautification, household fruit production, and public landscape development. The article is written to show that nurseries are not only about trees; they are a long-term investment in livelihoods, environmental recovery, and the visual transformation of towns and public institutions across Somaliland.
Draft Article 03
What Somaliland and Kenya Can Learn from Each Other in Dryland Agriculture
This cross-border draft article compares Somaliland's field realities with lessons from Kenya's dryland and semi-arid farming zones. It highlights irrigation efficiency, farmer training, cooperative organisation, and market-oriented production as shared priorities. Written in a professional but readable tone, the piece positions Somaliland and Kenya as valuable learning partners in sustainable agriculture, resilience planning, and regional food systems development.

About Summary

Abdiwahab Ismail Abdi is an agricultural development professional serving as Director of Plant Cultivation and Landscape Development at the Somaliland Ministry of Agriculture and Development. His work brings together agricultural policy, field implementation, climate-resilient crop systems, nursery development, landscape greening, and farmer capacity building. With experience across Somaliland and relevance to dryland systems in Kenya and the wider Horn of Africa, he focuses on practical, locally grounded solutions that improve productivity, resilience, and environmental restoration.
